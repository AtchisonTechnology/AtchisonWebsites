# All I Want for Christmas Is...

*An architect's wish list for 2027. None of it comes in a box.*

---

Architects are hard to shop for.

We don't need another monitor or another conference hoodie. What we want can't be wrapped, and most of it has to be built by the people we work with.

So this year I made a list. A wish list. Six wishes, each one small enough you can give me right away in January.

And each wish comes with its own Christmas carol.

🎶*All I want for Christmas is these six things...*🎶

Yeah, yeah. I know. My apologies in advance. But here we go.

## An owner for every service

I'd like every service in production to have exactly one team that answers for it.

A Slack channel doesn't count. Neither does "platform, probably." I mean one team, with its name in the service catalog and the members of the team on the pager rotation.

When something breaks at night, the fix is rarely the hard part. Finding someone who knows enough to make it is. I wrote about that in [When Everything Is Critical, Nothing Is](https://softwarearchitectureinsights.com/posts/when-everything-is-critical-nothing-is?utm_source=sai-email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=all-i-want-for-christmas&utm_content=body). Naming an owner is the cheapest reliability improvement most companies never make.

If you only grant me one wish on this list, grant me this one.

🎶*Away in a manger, no owner, no team...*🎶

No service should spend the holidays without an owner.

## Permission to turn something off

Every company has a billing-export-v2. It runs on three instances, and nobody dares to stop it because nobody recognizes the name.

I want someone to be thanked for retiring it. Out loud, in a meeting, with their manager in the room.

Turning old systems off saves money and leaves on-call with less to worry about. The work is invisible until something goes wrong, though, so it never makes anybody's to-do list. [Nobody Gets Promoted for Turning Things Off](https://softwarearchitectureinsights.com/posts/nobody-gets-promoted-for-turning-things-off?utm_source=sai-email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=all-i-want-for-christmas&utm_content=body) lays out a safe way to start.

This is an easy wish to grant me. You can use the savings to buy me a coffee next week.

🎶*Fast away the old service passes, fa la la la la...*🎶

## An evaluation set someone actually owns

Plenty of teams built an evaluation set for their AI features this year. Far fewer have touched it since.

The cases still reflect what users asked in the spring. The engineer who wrote them moved to another team, and the model changed underneath them over the summer.

My wish is small. One named owner for each eval set, and a recurring reminder to refresh the cases every quarter. That's the argument in [Someone Has to Own Evaluation](https://softwarearchitectureinsights.com/posts/someone-has-to-own-evaluation?utm_source=sai-email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=all-i-want-for-christmas&utm_content=body). An eval set nobody owns is a number on a dashboard that nobody trusts.

🎶*Jingle bells, eval sets, rerun all the way...* 🎶

## A decision record that says why

Most architecture decision records (ADRs) tell you what was decided. We moved to events. We chose Postgres.

The record I want explains why. It says what else was on the table, what the team was worried about, and what would make them change their minds.

The code already tells you the what. The why walks out the door with the person who knew it. [Designing Systems for Architects You Haven't Hired Yet](https://softwarearchitectureinsights.com/posts/designing-systems-for-architects-you-havent-hired-yet?utm_source=sai-email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=all-i-want-for-christmas&utm_content=body) is about writing it down before that day comes.

🎶*On the fourth ADR of Christmas my developers said to me.* 🎶

## A second architect, and one answer

A lot of companies have a single architect everybody waits on. We'd never accept a single point of failure like that in a database.

So I wish for a second architect. Then I wish for the harder thing, which is two architects giving the same answer to the same question.

Hiring solves the first wish. The second needs each architect to own a domain, and everyone to know who decides what. [When Your Architects Give Different Answers](https://softwarearchitectureinsights.com/posts/scaling-architectural-decisions?utm_source=sai-email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=all-i-want-for-christmas&utm_content=body) covers how to set that up.

🎶*We three architects of Orient are...*🎶

Three architects is fine. Three stars might be a bit of a problem.

## A boring on-call week

My last wish is the one the other five add up to.

A week where the pager goes off twice, both alerts are real, and whoever picks them up knows exactly whose service it is. No one spends forty minutes hunting for an owner.

Boring on-call is what good architecture feels like from the inside. Nobody writes a postmortem about it, and nobody brags about it at the holiday party. It's still the best gift a team can give itself.

🎶*Silent night... All is calm, all is bright...*🎶

## Start with one

You won't get all six in January, and you don't need to. A five-person startup where everyone owns everything, and everyone knows it, already has an owner for every service. Skip that wish and start with the decision records.

For everyone else, pick the wish that would have saved your team the most pain this year. Put a name next to it and a date on it.

Happy holidays. Next week, I'll look back at the calls I got wrong in 2026.
